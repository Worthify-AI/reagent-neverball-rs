# Reading the compiled visuals

The visual reconstruction used the compiled level and artwork as data, while the original game implementation source remained withheld. The renderer and simulation are independently authored. The matching licensed art explains the shared appearance; it is not evidence that the new engine reproduces every original rendering rule.

The SOL files begin with little-endian magic 0x4c4f53af and version 8. A 92-byte header supplies table counts. For Easy 01, 332 text bytes and twelve 8-byte dictionary records put the material table at byte 520. Eleven 136-byte material records are followed by vertex, edge, plane/normal, UV, corner-offset and triangle tables. Our extractor reaches byte 162716 exactly, the file length. Material records hold four RGBA float32 vectors, a shininess float, a flags integer and a 64-byte texture name. Generated Ghidra output at 0x17fc0 independently shows the magic/version check and those material reads. Its in-memory offsets are not interchangeable with file offsets.

Each 16-byte triangle contains a material ID and three indices into the corner-offset table. Each 12-byte corner record supplies a UV index, normal/plane index and vertex index. Resolving those references restores the authored texture alignment and per-vertex normals. The recovered positions for all 2,320 triangles exactly equal the existing simulation fixture. Material 8 maps 1,984 triangles to chrome rail geometry; material 4 maps the two main floor triangles to turf-green; material 9 maps two overlay triangles to arrow-green-light. The renderer adapts texture coordinates from(U,V) to(U,1−V), then repeats them with fract. The V flip is our image-coordinate adaptation; it is not a newly recovered physical constant.

The ball is a useful opacity example. Its material has diffuse RGBA (0.800000011920929,0.800000011920929,0.800000011920929,1.0): the approximately 80% RGB coefficients are not 80% opacity. The material alpha is 1.0, or 100%. Its LA PNG instead stores alpha 127 at every pixel: 127/255 =49.80392156862745%. That is a texture-alpha contribution. Final appearance also depends on lighting, overlapping sphere surfaces, depth handling and blending. Our renderer uses a standard source-alpha blend and approximate lighting, so we do not label its final ball as an exact 49.8%-opaque reproduction of the reference.

Easy 01 stores its ball position and radius at byte144556: (1.0,0.25049999356269836,−1.0,0.25). Its goal at byte144540 is(1.0,0.0,−17.0,0.75). Eighteen 20-byte item records begin at 144180. The dictionary text at byte228 says goal 10; time 9000 at byte218 is interpreted as 90 seconds from runtime. The reference configuration records 50° FOV. Our renderer uses that angle and the recovered ball radius; camera following, sky-gradient framing, light direction, specular weights, coin tint and HUD opacity are calibrated or independently chosen. The calibration register labels each of these separately.

The retained evidence frames are genuine captures from the reference recording and the final native Rust capture at 0, 0.4, 2.6, 5.5, 8.5, 10 and 14 seconds. The native trace logs after its simulation update, while rendering occurs before the update, so screenshot annotations use the preceding logged state. Reference positions are the nearest replay samples using the existing 2.5222222222-second start alignment, and are explicitly approximate video-to-state matches. These runs collect zero coins: they demonstrate forward movement and braking on the starting lane, not a completed level. The frames are useful precisely because they also expose the remaining camera, filtering, transparency and HUD differences.


The public converter is `harness/convert_visual_sol.py`; converted records are in `fixtures/*-visual.json`. Asset hashes, origins, copyrights, preferred forms and license notices are in `assets/neverball/`. Licensed art was reused. Game implementation source was withheld during construction.
